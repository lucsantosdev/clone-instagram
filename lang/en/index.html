Visual Hierarchy

The new version increases the contrast between information levels: title, form, and secondary actions. This reduces initial cognitive load and improves scanability, especially for returning users.

  • Better separation between brand content and main task (login).
  • Greater predictability in reading order (F/Z pattern).
  • Clearer use of typographic weights to guide attention.

Legibility and Visual Comfort

The redesign prioritizes larger touch areas, generous spacing, and soft contrast, balancing accessibility with brand aesthetics.

  • Taller fields with better clickable area.
  • Line-height and spacing between blocks favor continuous reading.
  • Footer with better organization for institutional navigation.

Primary Actions and Feedback

The primary action ("Login") is highlighted by color and size, while alternative actions have a consistent secondary style, facilitating quick decisions.

  • Visually dominant primary CTA.
  • Support actions positioned after the main task.
  • Predictable button pattern to reduce click errors.

Consistency and Interface System

The new version shows greater consistency in radius, spacing, and components, bringing the implementation closer to a sustainable design system for product evolution.

  • More coherent typographic scales across sections.
  • Uniform visual rhythm in fields and buttons.
  • Greater responsive adaptation without layout breakage.

Observed Best Practices

Useful guidelines for applying in digital products with a focus on conversion and clarity.

1. Prioritize a primary action

The primary CTA should have higher contrast and scale than other interactive elements.

2. Work density with intention

Consistent spacing improves readability and reduces the perceived effort to complete tasks.

3. Maintain visual consistency

Standardized radius, component height, and typography increase trust and predictability.

4. Ensure real responsiveness

Fluid layouts and strategic breakpoints preserve the experience across different devices.
